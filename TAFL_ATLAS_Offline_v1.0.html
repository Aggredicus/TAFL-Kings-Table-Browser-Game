Rules and visual provenance

What history tells us—and what it does not.

Evidence tiers

Attested: named historic description, illustrated board or extant artifact.

Reconstructed: a modern playable rule set inferred from incomplete records.

Contemporary: modern competitive additions, explicitly dated when known.

The illustrated board and ornaments are original interpretations rather than replicas. Culturally specific symbolism is used conservatively.

Mechanics and limitations

Normal movement, custodian capture, restricted throne/corners, side-specific king capture, shieldwall, automatic escape and no-move rulings are calculated locally.

Copenhagen exit fort and encirclement: these advanced strategic outcomes require a two-player manual agreement using Special win claim; the program does not pretend to prove that a formation cannot be broken. Computer games cannot auto-adjudicate those claims.

Alea Evangelii: the starting-position template is an interpretive 48/24 arrangement, not a verified transcription of the manuscript's specific markers; play rules are a documented modern adaptation.

Artwork reasoning

Norse Borre references linked ring-chain and interlaced animal forms; Irish Insular craft suggests spirals and pegged yew-wood boards; Welsh and Scottish themes use restrained stone-carving inspired palettes; Anglo-Saxon uses an illuminated manuscript aesthetic.

Sámi Tablut uses a hide/antler/embroidery material interpretation informed by the 1732 account. It intentionally avoids reproducing culturally restricted patterns or treating modern invented icons as historical Sámi religious symbols.
